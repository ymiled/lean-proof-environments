\title{\textbf{Measuring Proof-Success Decay with Dependency Depth}\\[2mm]
\large A verifiable-reward environment over a machine-checked security metatheorem}
\author{Youssef Miled}
\date{August 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
A published claim holds that when proofs are produced without compositional
structure, model success falls by ``roughly a 3x reduction in successful problem
count for every additional dependency solved.'' That constant justifies treating
verified dependencies as trusted interfaces rather than re-proving them. It was
measured once, on one corpus, and has not been independently checked. We build
an open Lean~4 environment to test it: a dependency graph of lemmas, two
conditions differing only in whether ancestors are supplied, and a grader that
audits which axioms a result depends on rather than whether a file compiles. We
report three findings. First, a measured decay of $1.73\times$ per depth on an
arithmetic ladder, which we argue \emph{cannot} be interpreted as a depth
effect, since the same data fits $1.44\times$ per lemma at an identical $R^2$.
Second, a clean qualitative separation between the two conditions on a
security-type-system ladder whose soundness theorem we machine-check end to end.
Third, and most consequential for anyone building such an environment, that the
binding constraint is difficulty calibration rather than grading or task
generation. We also report several negative results, including two ladder
designs that silently measured nothing and a safeguard that was inert for the
entire life of a family.
\end{abstract}

\section{The claim under test}

Theorem's \texttt{lf-lean} reports that monolithic translation degrades
exponentially in dependency count, at ``roughly a 3x reduction in successful
problem count for every additional dependency solved,'' and estimates that a
monolithic approach ``would yield less than 20\% success.'' The figure is the
justification for compositional verification, in which already-verified
dependencies are supplied as trusted interfaces, reducing context from
$O(|P|)$ to $O(\max_i |c_i|)$.

We test it on independent corpora with an open grader.

\section{The environment}

\paragraph{Ladders.} A \emph{family} is a Lean theory plus a dependency DAG of
lemmas. Depth is derived from the graph, never declared: $\mathrm{depth}(L)=1$ if
$L$ has no dependencies, else $1+\max$ over its dependencies. A rung is a schema
whose identifiers are placeholders, so each seed yields a freshly renamed theory.

\paragraph{Conditions.} A task is a \emph{set} of lemmas to prove; everything
else in the family is supplied already proved. Compositional is the singleton
$\{L\}$; monolithic is $\{L\}\cup\mathrm{ancestors}(L)$. At depth 5 that is the
difference between three lines and eight lemmas.

\paragraph{Grader.} Acceptance requires that the file compiles, that
\texttt{\#print axioms} on every target lies within
$\{\texttt{propext},\texttt{Classical.choice},\texttt{Quot.sound}\}$, and that no
banned syntax appears. The axiom audit is load-bearing, not decorative. During
development a rung failed to prove; Lean reported the error but \emph{still
declared the theorem}, and every downstream lemma citing it compiled with exit
status 0 and no diagnostics. Only the axiom check revealed
\texttt{sorryAx}. A grader asking ``did it compile?'' would have scored that
entire subtree as proved.

Statement drift is prevented structurally rather than detected: the harness
writes the \texttt{theorem \ldots := by} header and the policy supplies only a
tactic block, so a model can never weaken or restate the goal.

\paragraph{Certification.} Before any sweep, \texttt{selftest} checks three
properties per rung: solvable compositionally, solvable monolithically, and that
each declared dependency is \emph{real}, verified by compiling the rung with its
ancestors absent from the file entirely.

\section{Families}

\begin{table}[h]\centering
\begin{tabular}{lrrl}
\toprule
family & rungs & depths & top rung \\
\midrule
\texttt{arithmetic} & 15 & 1--5 & commutativity of multiplication \\
\texttt{noninterference} & 12 & 1--4 & soundness of a security type system \\
\texttt{vsi} & 14 & 1--5 & soundness, with subtyping, loops, functions \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The third family is not invented for the benchmark. It is the lemma chain of a
Lean~4 formalisation of a security type system in the Volpano--Smith--Irvine
style, extended with a nesting-depth-indexed command type $\mathsf{Ncmd}(\tau,n)$
and with functions. Its soundness theorem is machine-checked end to end:

\begin{lstlisting}
theorem noninterference_full (hft : FTOk G sg ft) (hty : TyC G sg c ty)
    (hst : lowEq G s t) (hs : evalC ft k s c = some s')
    (ht : evalC ft k t c = some t') : lowEq G s' t'
\end{lstlisting}

784 lines, no \texttt{sorryAx}, axioms within $\{\texttt{propext},
\texttt{Quot.sound}\}$. Semantics is fuel-indexed, so the theorem is
termination-insensitive: it relates runs that both finish. Expression agreement
and command noninterference are proved \emph{simultaneously} by induction on
fuel, because a call inside an expression runs a command and an assignment
inside a command evaluates an expression, so neither can be established first.

Two properties make this a better ladder than a synthetic one. The depth is the
dependency structure the proof actually has, so it cannot be accused of having
been shaped to produce a curve. And contamination is near zero: Peano arithmetic
is in every training set, this system is in none.

\section{Results}

\paragraph{A decay constant, and why it does not identify a mechanism.}
On \texttt{arithmetic}, monolithic pass rates for Haiku~4.5 fall
$1.00, 0.75, 0.33, 0.00, 0.00$ across depths 1--5, while Sonnet~5 remains at
$1.00$ until failing the depth-5 task (8 lemmas). Fitting $\log(\text{pass})$
against depth on the non-zero points gives

\[
1.73\times \text{ per depth} \qquad (R^2 = 0.93).
\]

Refitting the \emph{identical data} against lemma count gives $1.44\times$ per
lemma at $R^2 = 0.930$, the same to three decimals. The two models are
statistically indistinguishable. This is the collinearity problem made concrete:
across our ladders, $\mathrm{corr}(\text{depth},\text{monolithic length})
\approx 0.98$. Worse, the source paper itself reports a difficulty cliff at
``17+ marginal LoC,'' and in our arithmetic ladder monolithic length crosses 17
exactly between depths 3 and 4. \textbf{Their own length cliff predicts our curve
with no depth effect existing at all.} We therefore do not present $1.73\times$
as a replication.

\paragraph{Condition separation on the metatheorem ladder.}
On \texttt{vsi}, with Sonnet~5 as a one-shot policy and no compiler access:

\begin{table}[h]\centering
\begin{tabular}{lrrrr}
\toprule
depth & compositional & $n$ & monolithic & $n$ \\
\midrule
1 & 0.83 & 6  & 0.83 & 12 \\
2 & 0.50 & 2  & 0.00 & 4 \\
3 & 0.75 & 12 & 0.00 & 3 \\
4 & 0.50 & 6  & 0.00 & 2 \\
5 & 1.00 & 3  & --   & 0 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The compositional arm holds between 0.50 and 1.00 at every depth; the monolithic
arm collapses to zero from depth 2 onward. The depth-5 compositional cell at
$1.00$ is instructive rather than anomalous: \texttt{noninterference\_full} is a
one-line application of \texttt{agree}, which the compositional condition
supplies. Depth and difficulty come apart precisely when the interfaces are
handed over, which is the effect compositional verification is supposed to
produce.

\paragraph{Decomposition changes what is provable.}
On an earlier version of the \texttt{noninterference} family, both
\texttt{confinement} and the soundness theorem failed at $0.00$ \emph{even
compositionally}, with every ancestor supplied. After splitting the 37-line
soundness proof into two intermediate lemmas, dropping the target to 22 lines,
the same policy at the same one-shot budget proved it. Nothing about the theorem,
the language, or the model changed; only the granularity of the trusted
interfaces did.

\section{Threats to validity}

\paragraph{Power.} Cells range from $n=2$ to $n=12$; the intended $k\ge5$ per
cell was not reached because an API session limit halted the sweep at 50 of 140
tasks. No decay constant should be fitted to the \texttt{vsi} table.

\paragraph{Noise floor.} Depth-1 rungs have no ancestors, so their monolithic and
compositional prompts are \emph{byte-identical}, verified by \texttt{diff}. They
are nonetheless answered by independent policy instances. In one sweep they
scored 8/8 in one arm and 6/8 in the other, putting run-to-run variance near
$25\%$. Depth-1 rungs therefore act as a built-in replication control that
measures the noise floor for free, and any single-draw cell must be read against
it.

\paragraph{Contamination.} Renaming blocks verbatim recall of library proof terms
but not strategy transfer, and the defence is itself untested. The \texttt{vsi}
family largely sidesteps this by not existing in any corpus.

\paragraph{Identification.} The causal question is open. A chain-versus-antichain
contrast at matched volume was designed to settle it: compare $k$ mutually
independent lemmas against $k$ chained ones, holding count and length fixed.
Equal pass rates implicate volume; a gap implicates chaining. It has only been
run against a policy that ceilings on it.

\section{Negative results}

These cost the most and are the least reproducible, so we record them.

\textbf{Closed terms collapse depth.} A ladder stating lemmas at a concrete point
($\texttt{op}_i\,1\,y = 1$) produced goals that were closed terms, so
\texttt{simp} evaluated rather than reasoned about them. A depth-3 rung fell to
\texttt{simp\_all} with nothing in scope. Rungs must quantify over the variables
the definitions recurse on.

\textbf{Uniformity destroys essential depth.} A family purpose-built so the chain
and antichain arms would match exactly failed, because each rung was the previous
one composed once more, so the chain was bypassable by unfolding. Matching by
construction requires uniformity, and uniformity makes the chain decorative. The
two requirements are contradictory; matched pairs must be searched for, not
constructed.

\textbf{A funnel admits no antichains.} Withheld sets must be up-closed, so
antichains can only be drawn from maximal elements. A family with one maximal
rung cannot support the identification experiment at all, regardless of size.

\textbf{An inert safeguard is worse than none.} The depth check certified the
\texttt{vsi} family green while four of its rungs declared dependencies their
proofs never cite. It graded a monolithic task using the compositional solution,
but there the ancestors are themselves targets, so missing blocks were filled
with \texttt{sorry} and the audit rejected the attempt for the wrong reason. The
check could never fire. It was found by hand-inspecting a dependency that looked
implausible. Every check should be tested against a case it is meant to reject.

\section{Conclusion}

The grader worked, the dependency machinery worked, the renaming worked, the
certifications worked once repaired. The binding constraint was none of these.
It was \textbf{difficulty calibration}: \texttt{arithmetic} ceilings for a strong
policy, the first \texttt{noninterference} family floored above depth 1, and only
a weaker policy or a rebuilt ladder landed in the band where a curve exists.

A task that always passes and a task that always fails both yield zero gradient.
That is the same sparse-reward failure attributed to unit tests. Dense signal is
therefore not a property of proof assistants; it is a property of a task
distribution matched to a policy. Proof assistants make dense signal possible,
not automatic, and anyone planning to scale reinforcement learning on
verification needs a calibration story, not just a kernel.

\paragraph{Artifact.} Environment, grader, three families, the machine-checked
metatheorem, and a design log of every failure above.
