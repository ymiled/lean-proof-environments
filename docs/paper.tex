\title{\textbf{A calibrated verifiable-reward environment for Lean proof tasks}}
\author{Youssef Miled}
\date{}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Scaling reinforcement learning on program verification requires an environment,
and environments are the scarce half. Existing code-generation environments rely
on programmatic rewards or model grading, and unit tests in particular provide
sparse feedback because coverage correlates poorly with bugs found. A proof
assistant removes both problems, but a kernel alone is not an environment: it
supplies a trustworthy reward without supplying a task distribution, a difficulty
knob, or any guarantee that the tasks sit where a policy can learn from them. We
build and characterise such an environment for Lean~4. It offers dense
kernel-derived reward with an axiom audit, a difficulty knob given by how many
dependencies are withheld, and reuse through supplied lemmas, corresponding to
the dense-signal, unbounded-complexity and scalable-reuse properties that a
verification RL environment is expected to have. To obtain a corpus free of
training-set contamination we machine-check the soundness theorem of a security
type system with subtyping, loops and functions, and use its lemma chain as a
ladder. Measurements support compositional verification: pooling tasks with at
least one dependency, supplying dependencies yields $16/23$ successes against
$0/9$ when they are withheld (Fisher exact $p=8.2\times10^{-4}$). Our main
contribution is a calibration methodology. We show that the binding constraint on
such environments is neither grading nor task generation but placing tasks in the
band where a policy sometimes succeeds, we give an instrument that measures the
environment's own noise floor for free, and we show that a naive decay sweep
cannot separate dependency depth from proof volume, together with a design that
can.
\end{abstract}

\section{Environments are the missing half}

The argument for verification as a reinforcement learning target is well made
elsewhere and we take it as given: proof assistants supply dense, ground-truth
feedback, verification tasks have no complexity ceiling, and solved lemmas become
reusable interfaces so a curriculum compounds. The gap is that this describes a
\emph{potential} environment rather than an existing one.

A proof corpus is not an environment. It has no difficulty parameter, generates
no fresh tasks, and is present in model training data. A kernel is not an
environment either: it scores a candidate but says nothing about which candidates
to pose. What is needed in between is a task distribution with a knob, a grader
that cannot be fooled, and evidence that the tasks land where learning signal
exists. This paper contributes that middle layer and, more importantly, a method
for checking that the third property actually holds, which we found to be the
part that fails silently.

\section{Design}

\paragraph{Ladders, and derived depth.}
A family is a Lean theory with a dependency graph over its lemmas. Depth is
computed from the graph rather than declared: depth $1$ for a lemma with no
dependencies, otherwise one more than its deepest dependency. Derivation is not
pedantry; hand-maintained graphs drift, and Section~\ref{sec:cal} reports a case
where four of fourteen lemmas carried dependencies their proofs never cite.

\paragraph{The difficulty knob.}
A task is a set of lemmas to prove, with every other lemma supplied already
proved. Withholding nothing gives the \emph{compositional} condition, a single
lemma with all ancestors available. Withholding everything beneath the target
gives the \emph{monolithic} condition. Intermediate sets interpolate, which is the
knob: difficulty is set by how much of the dependency cone the policy must
reconstruct. This is also the environment's reuse property, since a supplied lemma
is exactly a trusted interface.

\paragraph{Reward.}
Acceptance requires that the file compiles, that \texttt{\#print axioms} on every
target lies within
$\{\texttt{propext},\texttt{Classical.choice},\texttt{Quot.sound}\}$, and that the
policy's text contains no banned tactic. Multi-lemma tasks are graded
all-or-nothing, matching the quantity of interest, which is problems solved.

The axiom audit is not redundant with compilation, and we recommend it to anyone
building a similar environment. During development a lemma failed to prove; Lean
reported the error and \emph{still declared the theorem}. Every downstream lemma
citing it compiled with exit status $0$ and no diagnostics of its own. Only
\texttt{\#print axioms}, three rungs later, revealed \texttt{sorryAx}. A reward
keyed on compilation would have paid out for that entire subtree.

\paragraph{Reward hacking, removed structurally.}
The policy never writes the theorem statement. The harness emits the
\texttt{theorem \ldots := by} header and splices in a tactic block only. A model
therefore cannot weaken, restate, or substitute the goal, which eliminates the
largest class of specification gaming without any statement-comparison logic. We
implemented that comparison first and deleted it.

\paragraph{Certification.}
No sweep runs until every rung is verified solvable in both conditions and every
declared dependency is verified real, the latter by compiling the rung with its
ancestors absent from the file entirely.

\section{A corpus that is not in the training data}

Two of our three families are theories written for the purpose, a Peano
development and a small security type system. Both are textbook material and a
model may well have memorised proofs of them.

For the third we machine-checked, in Lean~4, the soundness theorem of a security
type system in the Volpano-Smith-Irvine style, extended with subtyping, while
loops, and functions. The formalisation is 784 lines with no \texttt{sorry};
axioms are confined to \texttt{propext} and \texttt{Quot.sound}. Semantics is
fuel-indexed, making the theorem termination-insensitive, and expression agreement
must be proved simultaneously with command noninterference by induction on fuel,
because a call inside an expression executes a command while an assignment inside
a command evaluates an expression.

Its lemma chain becomes the third ladder: 14 lemmas, depths 1--5, from lattice
properties up to soundness. Two properties recommend it. The depths are the
dependency structure a real metatheorem has, rather than a gradient chosen to
produce a curve. And the system exists in no public corpus, so measurements on it
are not confounded by recall.

\section{Measurements}

Policies answer one-shot with no compiler access. The reference solution scores
$1.00$ in every cell and a trivial \texttt{rfl} policy scores $0.00$, bounding the
environment and confirming the grader discriminates.

Pooling the tasks where the conditions genuinely differ, namely depth $\ge 2$,
gives compositional $16/23$ ($[0.49,0.84]$) against monolithic $0/9$
($[0.00,0.30]$), Fisher exact two-sided $p = 8.2\times10^{-4}$. On a corpus with
no contamination and a kernel-checked reward, trusted interfaces are worth a great
deal, which is the empirical content of the compositional-verification argument.

The depth-5 compositional cell at $1.00$ illustrates the mechanism rather than
contradicting it: that theorem is a one-line application of the lemma beneath it,
which the compositional condition supplies. Depth stops predicting difficulty
exactly when the interfaces are handed over.

\subsection{An instrument for the environment's own noise}

Depth-1 lemmas have no ancestors, so their prompts are byte-identical across the
two conditions; we confirm this with \texttt{diff}. They are nevertheless answered
by independent policy instances under different labels. In one sweep those
identical prompts scored $8/8$ in one arm and $6/8$ in the other, placing
run-to-run variance near $25\%$.

This is worth building in deliberately. Any environment of this shape can include
tasks that are duplicated by construction, giving a free per-sweep estimate of its
own noise floor, against which single-sample cells can be judged.

\section{Calibration is the binding constraint}
\label{sec:cal}

The property a verification environment is assumed to have is dense signal. In
practice we found dense signal is not conferred by the kernel. It is conferred by
a task distribution matched to the policy, and it fails silently in three
distinguishable ways.

\paragraph{Ceiling.} The Peano ladder is solved at every depth by a strong model.
Reward is constant, gradient is zero, and the environment teaches nothing.

\paragraph{Floor.} The first version of our security ladder failed at every depth
above 1, including in the compositional condition where all ancestors were
supplied. Also zero gradient. The repair was to split a 37-line proof into two
intermediate lemmas, dropping the target to 22 lines, after which the same policy
at the same budget proved the theorem it had previously failed. Nothing about the
mathematics changed, only the granularity of the interfaces, which is a concrete
demonstration that decomposition changes what is provable at fixed compute.

\paragraph{Structural.} Some measurements are impossible in a given graph rather
than merely underpowered. Withheld sets must be upward closed, so sets of mutually
independent lemmas can only be drawn from maximal elements; a family with a single
maximal element admits none at any size.

\paragraph{Silent certification failure.} Our dependency-reality check was vacuous
for multi-lemma tasks: it graded a monolithic task using the single-lemma
solution, so missing blocks were filled with \texttt{sorry} and the audit rejected
the attempt for the wrong reason, leaving the failure branch unreachable. It
reported success for the whole life of one family while four of its fourteen
lemmas declared dependencies their proofs never cite, inflating depths. Checks
should be exercised against cases they are meant to reject.

We take the general lesson to be that environment quality is an empirical property
requiring instrumentation, not a consequence of using a kernel.

\section{Measuring decay without confounding it}

A natural use of this environment is to fit how success decays with dependency
depth. We caution that the obvious sweep does not identify the quantity.

On a general-mathematics ladder since removed from the benchmark (see
limitations), Haiku~4.5 monolithic pass rates fall $1.00, 0.75, 0.33,
0.00, 0.00$ over depths 1--5. Regressing $\log(\text{pass})$ on depth over the
non-zero points gives $1.73\times$ per depth at $R^2=0.930$. Regressing the same
observations on \emph{lemma count} gives $1.44\times$ per lemma at $R^2=0.930$.
The fits are indistinguishable, because monolithic length is by construction the
sum over ancestors and the two regressors correlate at $0.98$ across our families.
A curve of this shape is equally consistent with dependency chains compounding and
with there simply being more to write.

The separating design is a matched contrast: $k$ mutually independent lemmas
against $k$ chained lemmas, holding both count and total reference length fixed.
Equal success implicates volume; a gap implicates chaining. Constructing such
pairs by making all rungs uniform does not work, since a chain of composed
identical steps is bypassable by unfolding, which we verified by compiling a
depth-4 lemma with the two beneath it deleted. Pairs must instead be searched for
among naturally occurring lemmas. We locate exactly length-matched pairs at
$k=2,3$ and provide the enumeration, but have so far run the contrast only against
a policy that ceilings on it.

\section{Limitations}

Cells contain between 2 and 12 attempts; an API rate limit halted our sweep at 50
of 140 planned tasks. Only the pooled comparison of Section~4.1 is adequately
powered, and no constant should be fitted to Table~\ref{tab:vsi}. Results cover
two model families.

Episodes are single-step, with one attempt and one binary reward. Per-tactic
actions with intermediate proof states as observations would require a persistent
Lean server rather than one-shot compilation, and are the natural next version.

We do not train. This is the environment half, with a frozen policy and a
\texttt{reset}/\texttt{step} interface to which no learner has yet been attached.
We regard the calibration diagnostics above as a precondition for doing so
usefully rather than as a substitute.

\section*{Reproducibility}

We release the environment, the grader, three ladders, the 784-line
machine-checked formalisation, and a log of the design failures in
Section~\ref{sec:cal}. A family is certified before any sweep. Sweeps split into
prompt export and grading so policies without an HTTP API can be evaluated, and
the runner emits a safe partition of tasks across policy instances, since a
compositional prompt supplies its target's ancestors together with their proofs
and therefore leaks answers if batched carelessly.
