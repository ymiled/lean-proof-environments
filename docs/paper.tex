\title{\textbf{A verifiable-reward environment for information-flow security,\\
with dependency structure recovered by necessity}}
\author{Youssef Miled}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Proof assistants are an attractive reward signal for reinforcement learning
because a kernel is ground truth rather than a proxy. A kernel is not an
environment, however: it scores a candidate without supplying a task
distribution, a difficulty knob, or any evidence that tasks sit where a policy
can learn. We build that middle layer for information-flow security in Lean~4.
Difficulty is set by how much of a lemma's dependency cone is withheld, reward
is kernel acceptance plus an axiom audit, and specification gaming is removed
structurally by never letting the policy write the theorem statement. Two
findings drive the design. First, the dependency structure that defines
difficulty \emph{cannot} be read from the Lean environment: theorem bodies are
elaborated asynchronously and discarded, so every theorem appears
dependency-free. We recover the graph instead by \emph{necessity}, asserting an
edge only when deleting the ancestor makes the proof fail, which is strictly
stronger than reading the proof term because a name can be mentioned without
being load-bearing. Second, hand-authored graphs are unreliable in practice: an
earlier version of one family carried four fabricated edges that inflated its
depths, and the self-check meant to catch them was structurally inert. The
resulting corpus spans 14 theories, 158 lemmas and 2{,}607 distinct task shapes,
every edge machine-established and every rung certified solvable. It includes a
784-line machine-checked soundness proof for a security type system with
subtyping, loops and functions, which is absent from public corpora and so is
free of the recall confound. Measurements support compositional verification, but weakly and with an
instructive caveat: pooling tasks with at least one dependency, supplying
dependencies yields $18/27$ successes against $4/14$ when withheld (Fisher exact
$p=0.026$). Counting duplicate policy responses as separate observations instead
gives $p=0.114$. The conclusion therefore turns on a single analysis decision,
which is what an underpowered benchmark looks like from the inside, and we
report both numbers rather than the favourable one. Our central practical claim is that the binding
constraint on such environments is neither grading nor task generation but
calibration, and we give instrumentation for it, including a noise-floor
estimator that costs nothing.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The case for verification as an RL target is by now familiar. Reward from a
proof kernel is objective, cannot be gamed at the level of logic, and unlike
unit-test reward it does not degrade as a proxy when coverage and correctness
diverge. Solved lemmas become reusable interfaces, so a curriculum compounds
rather than saturating.

What that argument describes is a \emph{potential} environment. A proof corpus
is not one: it has no difficulty parameter, generates no fresh tasks, and sits
in model training data. A kernel is not one either: it grades a candidate but
says nothing about which candidates to pose. The missing layer is a task
distribution with a knob, a grader that cannot be fooled, and evidence that the
tasks land where learning signal exists. We build that layer, and we report that
the third requirement is the one that fails silently.

Our contributions:

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{An environment} for information-flow security verification in
Lean~4, with a difficulty knob given by withheld dependencies, kernel reward
with an axiom audit, and structural rather than detective protection against
specification gaming (\S\ref{sec:env}).
\item \textbf{Dependency extraction by necessity} (\S\ref{sec:extract}). We
first show that the obvious method is unavailable: in Lean~4.32 theorem proof
terms are unreachable after elaboration through every accessor we tried, so
graph extraction reports max depth~1. We replace it with a deletion test that is
stronger than term inspection, and validate it against four dependencies known
to be fabricated.
\item \textbf{A generated corpus} of 14 IFC theories, 158 lemmas, 2{,}607 task
shapes, every edge machine-established and every rung certified solvable
(\S\ref{sec:corpus}), including a machine-checked metatheorem that is absent
from public corpora.
\item \textbf{Empirical results} supporting compositional verification, a
noise-floor instrument that is free by construction, and the argument that
calibration is the binding constraint (\S\ref{sec:exp}, \S\ref{sec:cal}).
\item \textbf{Four benchmark-design failures} documented in enough detail to be
avoided (\S\ref{sec:neg}). Each was caught by a check rather than by reading,
and one was caught only because a check that had always reported success was
itself found to be incapable of failing.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Positioning}

We are aware of no released environment for verification RL that supplies a
task distribution with a difficulty parameter rather than a fixed corpus. The
closest published effort we are testing against is Theorem's \texttt{lf-lean},
which translates the 1{,}276 statements of \emph{Logical Foundations} from Rocq
to Lean using frontier models with proof-assistant feedback, and reports that
success without compositional structure falls by roughly a threefold factor per
additional dependency solved. That is a pipeline and a corpus rather than an
environment: it has no knob, generates no fresh tasks, and its corpus is public
and therefore in training data. Our object is the layer underneath it.

On scale, the relevant comparison is not to proof corpora, which are large, but
to \emph{benchmarks whose dependency structure is known}. Ladders of this kind
are ordinarily hand-authored and number in the dozens of lemmas, because
authoring a dependency graph by hand is slow and, as \S\ref{sec:neg} shows,
unreliable. Ours contains 158 lemmas across 14 theories yielding 2{,}607 task
shapes, and no edge in it was written by a human: every one was established by
deleting a lemma and observing the proof fail. The cost of that guarantee is
2.85\,s per task grade and roughly four minutes of extraction per twelve
theories, which is what makes machine-established structure practical at this
scale rather than merely desirable.

Two design commitments follow, both aimed at the environment being extended by
people who did not build it. Adding a theory must be one module and no edits
elsewhere, so a family is loaded from a specification plus an extracted graph
rather than from hand-written code, and a contributor cannot introduce a
dependency graph at all: the only way to add edges is to let the extractor find
them. And every check must be exercised against a case it is meant to reject
before it is trusted, a rule we adopted after finding one that had reported
success throughout its life and could never have reported anything else. Both
rules exist because the failure modes in \S\ref{sec:neg} are silent, and a
silent failure inherited by a later user is worse than one we merely suffered
ourselves.

\section{The environment}
\label{sec:env}

\paragraph{Ladders and derived depth.}
A \emph{family} is a Lean theory plus a dependency graph over its lemmas. Depth
is computed, never declared: $\mathrm{depth}(L)=1$ if $L$ has no dependencies,
otherwise $1+\max$ over them. \S\ref{sec:neg} explains why that distinction is
load-bearing rather than fussy.

\paragraph{The difficulty knob.}
A task is a \emph{set} of lemmas to prove; every other lemma in the family is
supplied already proved. Withholding only the target gives the
\emph{compositional} condition; withholding its whole cone gives the
\emph{monolithic} condition; intermediate withheld sets interpolate. The
constraint is that a withheld set must be up-closed within the cone, since a
supplied lemma carries a proof citing its own dependencies. This knob is also
the environment's reuse property: a supplied lemma is exactly a trusted
interface.

\paragraph{Reward.}
Acceptance requires that the file compiles, that \texttt{\#print axioms} on
every target lies within
$\{\texttt{propext},\texttt{Classical.choice},\texttt{Quot.sound}\}$, and that
the policy's text contains no banned tactic. Multi-lemma tasks are graded
all-or-nothing, matching the quantity of interest.

The axiom audit is not redundant with compilation, and we recommend it to
anyone building a similar environment. During development a lemma failed to
prove; Lean reported the error and \emph{still declared the theorem}. Every
downstream lemma citing it compiled with exit status $0$ and no diagnostics of
its own. Only \texttt{\#print axioms}, three rungs later, revealed
\texttt{sorryAx}. A reward keyed on compilation would have paid out for that
entire subtree.

\paragraph{Specification gaming, removed structurally.}
The policy never writes the theorem statement. The harness emits the
\texttt{theorem \ldots := by} header and splices in a tactic block only, so a
model cannot weaken, restate, or substitute the goal. We implemented a
statement-comparison check first and deleted it: making drift impossible is
better than detecting it. We verified the property holds for statements with
implicit binders, instance arguments and universe polymorphism.

\section{Dependency extraction by necessity}
\label{sec:extract}

\subsection{A negative result: the kernel will not tell you}

The natural way to obtain a true dependency graph is to ask Lean which constants
a proof references. This is not possible in Lean~4.32. Theorem bodies are
elaborated asynchronously and the term is not retained, so
\texttt{ConstantInfo.value?} yields \texttt{none}:

\begin{lstlisting}
theorem foo (n : Nat) : n = n := rfl
theorem bar (n : Nat) : n = n := foo n     -- cites foo

env.find? `bar                  -- value?.isSome = false
env.toKernelEnv.find? `bar      -- value?.isSome = false
liftCoreM (getConstInfo `bar)   -- value?.isSome = false
liftCoreM (collectAxioms `bar)  -- []
\end{lstlisting}

Two lines after \texttt{bar} is declared its proof is unreachable. Compiling to
\texttt{.olean} and loading through \texttt{withImportModules} gives the same
answer, so this is not staleness of an in-memory map. Only the statement's
constants survive. Run over our largest theory, extraction reported every
theorem as dependency-free with max depth~1, which is precisely the failure the
extractor existed to prevent, arriving from the opposite direction.

The type-level pass is still useful: it separates theory-internal constants from
external ones, which is the supplied-versus-withheld split, and identifies
auto-generated declarations that must never become tasks. Of 418 declarations in
that theory, 248 are auto-generated and 46 are human-written theorems.

\subsection{Necessity as a stronger criterion}

We assert an edge $T \to A$ only when removing $A$ from the file makes $T$'s
proof fail. This is strictly stronger than reading the proof term: a name can
appear in a term without being load-bearing, and necessity tests what the
difficulty knob actually depends on.

One subtlety makes it non-trivial. Removing $A$ also breaks every supplied lemma
that itself needs $A$, and those failures would be charged to $T$. We therefore
process rungs in source order, so the graph for everything earlier is already
known and the whole $A$-cone can be removed together. A failure then means $T$
needs something in that cone, hence transitively needs $A$. That yields the
transitive relation; direct edges follow by transitive reduction. Cost is one
compile per candidate edge, quadratic in rungs, and the inner loop parallelises.

\paragraph{Validation.} Against a family whose hand-written graph was known to
contain four fabricated edges, necessity extraction rejects exactly those four
and retains the real ones:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
declared edge & closure & verdict \\
\midrule
\texttt{lowEq\_symm $\to$ lowEq\_refl}   & $\emptyset$ & rejected \\
\texttt{lowEq\_trans $\to$ lowEq\_refl}  & $\emptyset$ & rejected \\
\texttt{Lvl.le\_trans $\to$ Lvl.le\_refl}& $\emptyset$ & rejected \\
\texttt{secure\_sub $\to$ Lvl.le\_refl}  & $\emptyset$ & rejected \\
\texttt{sub\_base\_inv $\to$ Lvl.le\_refl} & present & accepted \\

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Reported max depth drops from a declared 6 to a true 5. The three remaining
differences between extracted and declared graphs are all redundant edges that
transitive reduction correctly removes, and none changes a depth.

\section{Corpus}
\label{sec:corpus}

\paragraph{A theory that is not in the training data.}
We machine-checked in Lean~4 the soundness theorem of a security type system in
the Volpano-Smith-Irvine style with subtyping, \texttt{while} loops and a
function extension: 784 lines, no \texttt{sorry}, axioms confined to
\texttt{propext} and \texttt{Quot.sound}. Semantics is fuel-indexed, so the
theorem is termination-insensitive. Expression agreement and command
noninterference must be proved simultaneously by induction on fuel, since a call
inside an expression executes a command while an assignment inside a command
evaluates an expression. Its lemma chain is a ladder whose depths are the
dependency structure a real metatheorem has, and whose content appears in no
public corpus.

\paragraph{Generated theories.}
A security type system is parameterised. We generate over expression grammar
size, the branch convention of the conditional, and the evaluation order of
sequencing, each point yielding a Lean theory with its own soundness chain. The
proof strategy is deliberately \emph{not} parameterised: every instance is
discharged by one tactic skeleton, so a variant either compiles for structural
reasons or is rejected before entering the corpus. The generator may emit
theories that do not work; it may not ship them.

\paragraph{Certification and cost.}
Necessity extraction over the generated theories took 261\,s for 660 probes.
Compositional self-test over the generated corpus: 132 tasks, \textbf{0
failures}, 48.8\,s wall clock, 2.85\,s mean per task grade. Every rung is
certified solvable in the compositional condition using its real proof, and
every edge was established by deletion.

\paragraph{Contamination.}
The two roles are deliberate. Generated theories and the control family support
per-seed renaming of every identifier, so a model cannot pattern-match onto a
remembered proof. The machine-checked theory cannot be renamed without breaking
its own imports and does not need to be, since it exists in no public corpus.
Renaming removes verbatim recall, not knowledge; whether it removes anything
measurable is the subject of ongoing work and is currently untested, which we
state rather than assume.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

Policies are Claude Sonnet~5 and Claude Haiku~4.5, answering one-shot with no
compiler access, so no iteration against the kernel is possible. The reference solution scores $1.00$ in every cell and a
trivial \texttt{rfl} policy scores $0.00$, bounding the environment and
confirming the grader discriminates.

Every per-cell interval overlaps its partner, so no single depth supports a
claim. Pooling depths $2$ to $4$, where the conditions genuinely differ:
supplied $18/27$ ($[0.48,0.81]$) against withheld $4/14$ ($[0.12,0.55]$), Fisher
exact two-sided $p=0.026$.

\paragraph{The result is fragile, and we say how.}
Three analysis decisions each move it, and we made all three before seeing that
they mattered rather than after.

\emph{Duplicates.} Tasks were dispatched to policy instances in batches chosen
to stop one prompt leaking another's answer. In the supplied arm that batching
let one instance answer several samples of the same task, which it did
identically. Counting those copies as separate observations gives $p=0.114$;
counting distinct responses gives $p=0.026$. Copies are not independent draws,
so the second is correct, but a result that flips on this is not a robust one.

\emph{Depth 5.} An earlier partial sweep included a supplied depth-5 cell at
$5/5$ against an empty withheld cell, and reported $p=8.2\times10^{-4}$. That
cell contained one distinct response repeated five times, and no contrast is
definable against an arm with no data. Removing it costs most of the apparent
effect.

\emph{Sample size.} That same earlier sweep put the withheld arm at $0/9$ and we
wrote that it was at zero. It is not: with more data it reaches $4/14$. A cell
reading exactly zero at $n=9$ is a small sample on a boundary, not a floor.

We keep the claim at $p=0.026$ and state that it rests on 27 and 14
observations. What the environment demonstrates here is less the size of the
compositional effect than how easily an under-filled cell manufactures one.

The depth-5 compositional cell at $1.00$ illustrates the mechanism rather than
contradicting it: that theorem is a one-line application of the lemma beneath
it, which the compositional condition supplies. Depth stops predicting
difficulty exactly when interfaces are handed over.

\paragraph{A noise-floor instrument that costs nothing.}
Depth-1 lemmas have no ancestors, so their monolithic and compositional prompts
are byte-identical; we confirm this with \texttt{diff}. They are nonetheless
answered by independent policy instances under different labels. In one sweep
those identical prompts scored $8/8$ in one arm and $6/8$ in the other, placing
run-to-run variance near $25\%$. Any benchmark of this shape gets such an
estimate for free, and single-sample cells should be read against it.

\paragraph{Decay is not identifiable from a depth sweep.}
On a general-mathematics ladder we later removed from the benchmark, Haiku~4.5
monolithic pass rates fell $1.00, 0.75, 0.33, 0.00, 0.00$ over depths 1--5. Regressing
$\log(\text{pass})$ on depth over the non-zero points gives $1.73\times$ per
depth at $R^2=0.930$; regressing the same observations on \emph{lemma count}
gives $1.44\times$ per lemma at $R^2=0.930$. The fits are indistinguishable,
because monolithic length is by construction the sum over ancestors and the two
regressors correlate at $0.98$. A separating design exists, contrasting $k$
mutually independent lemmas against $k$ chained ones at matched count and
length, but it cannot be built by making all rungs uniform, since a chain of
composed identical steps is bypassable by unfolding (\S\ref{sec:neg}). Matched
pairs must be searched for.

\section{Calibration is the binding constraint}
\label{sec:cal}

Dense signal is usually treated as a property conferred by the kernel. It is
not. It is a property of a task distribution matched to a policy, and it fails
silently in three distinguishable ways.

\textbf{Ceiling.} A general-mathematics ladder was solved at every depth by a
strong model. Constant reward, zero gradient.

\textbf{Floor.} The first version of our security ladder failed at every depth
above~1, including compositionally with all ancestors supplied. Also zero
gradient. The repair was to split a 37-line proof into two intermediate lemmas,
dropping the target to 22 lines, after which the same policy at the same budget
proved a theorem it had previously failed. Nothing about the mathematics
changed; only the granularity of the interfaces did. Decomposition changes what
is provable at fixed compute, which is the compositional-verification claim in
miniature.

\textbf{Structural.} Some measurements are impossible in a given graph rather
than underpowered. Because withheld sets are up-closed, sets of mutually
independent lemmas can only be drawn from maximal elements; a family with one
maximal element admits none at any size.

A task that always succeeds and one that always fails both yield zero gradient,
which is the sparse-reward failure usually attributed to unit tests. Proof
assistants make dense feedback possible and do not make it automatic. Scaling RL
on verification needs a calibration story in addition to a kernel.

\section{Four failures worth documenting}
\label{sec:neg}

\textbf{Closed goals collapse depth.} Lemmas stated at a concrete point produce
goals that are closed terms, which \texttt{simp} evaluates rather than reasons
about. A depth-3 rung fell to \texttt{simp\_all} with no lemmas in scope. The
ladder compiled and measured nothing. Rungs must quantify over the variables the
definitions recurse on.

\textbf{Uniformity destroys essential depth.} A family built so chain and
independent arms would match exactly failed, because when each rung is the
previous one composed once more the chain is bypassable by unfolding
definitions and reusing the base lemma. We verified this by compiling the
depth-4 lemma with the two beneath it deleted. Exact matching requires
uniformity; uniformity makes dependencies decorative.

\textbf{An inert safeguard is worse than none.} The dependency-reality check was
vacuous for multi-lemma tasks: it graded a monolithic task using the
single-lemma solution, so missing blocks were filled with \texttt{sorry}, the
audit rejected the attempt for the wrong reason, and the failure branch was
unreachable. It reported success for the entire life of one family while four of
its fourteen lemmas declared dependencies their proofs never cite. It was found
by inspection, not by the check: a one-line proof cannot plausibly need a lemma.
Every check should be exercised against a case it is meant to reject.

\textbf{An impossible histogram exposed a counting bug.} Task shapes were
counted by enumerating up-closed subsets of a rung's cone, but up-closedness was
checked against every lemma in the family rather than only those rendered.
Lemmas above the target never appear in the file, so requiring them to be
satisfiable ruled out every rung with a dependent. The histogram showed zero
shapes at depths 2 and 3, which is impossible when rungs exist there, and that
impossibility is what surfaced the error.

\section{Limitations}

Sample sizes are small. After deduplication, cells hold between 2 and 15
distinct responses, and the sweep is incomplete at 73 of 140 planned tasks.
Depth~5 was dropped entirely because the withheld arm there was never collected.
Only the pooled comparison of \S\ref{sec:exp} carries weight, it reaches
$p=0.026$, and it does not survive counting duplicates as independent. Results
cover two model families.

Independence within cells was imperfect and is the reason deduplication matters.
Batching chosen to prevent answer leakage let one policy instance answer several
samples of a task in the supplied arm, which it did identically; distinct
responses per supplied cell are 4, 15 and 8 at depths 2 to 4 against nominal 10,
15 and 10. The withheld arm was dispatched one instance per sample index and is
unaffected. The fix is one instance per (task, sample) pair, which we did not do
throughout and which the runner should enforce rather than leave to whoever
launches the sweep.

Episodes are single-step: one attempt, one binary reward. Per-tactic actions
with intermediate proof states would need a persistent Lean server rather than
one-shot compilation. The renaming defence is untested; the corpus is built so
that it \emph{can} be tested, which is the immediate next experiment. The
machine-checked theorem is termination-insensitive, which is forced by fuel and
standard for languages with loops.

We do not train. This is the environment half, with a frozen policy and a
\texttt{reset}/\texttt{step} interface to which no learner has been attached. We
regard the calibration diagnostics above as a precondition for attaching one
rather than a substitute.

\section{Conclusion}

Building a verifiable-reward environment turns out to be mostly a problem of not
fooling yourself. The kernel is honest, but everything around it, the dependency
graph, the difficulty knob, the checks that certify them, the sweep that fills
the cells, can be quietly wrong in ways that still produce plausible tables.
Every substantive error here was caught by a check or by an impossible number,
and one was caught only after discovering that a check reporting success
throughout its life could never have reported anything else.

That pattern reached our own headline result. At one point this paper would have
reported a compositional effect at $p=8.2\times10^{-4}$, resting on a cell of
five identical responses and a comparison against an arm with no data. What it
reports now is $p=0.026$ on 27 and 14 observations, which does not survive
counting duplicate responses as independent. The effect is probably real and is
certainly not established, and the distance between those two statements was
three analysis decisions rather than any new experiment.

We think that is the honest lesson of building this. Calibration determines
whether an environment can teach anything, and the same under-filled cells that
give a policy no gradient give the experimenter a spurious result. The two
failures have one cause, and a benchmark that instruments the first should
instrument the second.

The environment, the corpus, the extraction procedure and the design log of
every failure above are released together, on the view that the failures are the
part hardest to reproduce.

\section*{Reproducibility}

Everything reported here is regenerable from the repository with a Lean~4
toolchain and no other dependency. \texttt{pdd.generate} writes the theory
specifications; \texttt{pdd.extract} recovers each dependency graph by deletion
and refuses to load one produced any other way; \texttt{pdd.selftest} certifies
a family and must report zero failures before a sweep is trusted;
\texttt{pdd.corpus} prints Table~\ref{tab:corpus}. Sweeps are split into prompt
export and grading so that policies without an HTTP API can be evaluated, and
the runner emits a safe partition of tasks across policy instances, because a
compositional prompt supplies its target's ancestors together with their proofs
and therefore leaks answers if batched carelessly. We violated that partition by
hand three times while collecting the results above, which is why the runner now
emits it rather than leaving it to judgement.

Wall-clock figures are from a laptop, single machine: 261\,s to extract twelve
generated theories (660 deletion probes), 48.8\,s to certify 132 compositional
tasks, 2.85\,s mean per task grade.
